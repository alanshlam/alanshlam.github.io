Chapter 10: The Double Gambit

The neon-lit student union at Cal State East Bay hummed with the high-energy chatter of the annual Autumn Cultural Exchange. Zoe Lin stood at the edge of the mezzanine, her hands gripped tightly around the cold iron railing as she watched the crowd below. Her eyes never left Xavier Stone.

She was adjusting a tiny, flesh-colored earpiece. Beside her in the shadows of the concrete pillar stood Jo Mercer, wearing a heavy leather jacket that concealed her shoulder sling, her eyes fixed on a ruggedized tablet displaying a live data stream.

Down on the floor, Xavier looked every bit the wealthy, slightly detached real estate mogul. He was wearing an expensive, dark-knit blazer, leaning elegantly on his black cane as he stood by the table of the Asian Pacific Islander American Public Affairs (APAPA) student association. Behind the table stood Vivian Fan. She was radiant, exuding a sharp, practiced charisma that had allowed her to rise meteorically through Bay Area political volunteer circles.

“He’s making contact,” Jo whispered into her collar mic.

Zoe held her breath as she watched Xavier offer Vivian a polite, winning smile. Through Zoe’s earpiece, Xavier’s voice came through with crystal clarity, captured by a high-gain directional microphone Jo had aimed from the balcony.

“I’ve spent my life building structures out of steel and concrete, Miss Fan,” Xavier was saying, his tone possessing a perfect blend of wealthy ambition and political naivety. “But Oakland is decaying. I want to build something sustainable. I’m seriously considering an independent run for Mayor.”

Vivian’s eyes lit up with a sudden, predatory focus—a look Zoe recognized instantly from Wendy’s files. Vivian was a textbook access agent, trained by her handlers to identify and cultivate up-and-coming local stars early in their careers. A prominent, wealthy architect launching a mayoral campaign was the ultimate target package.

“You’ve come to the right place, Mr. Stone,” Vivian said, her voice warm, inviting, and effortlessly persuasive. “APAPA and our campus coalitions represent the literal heartbeat of the East Bay’s immigrant and minority communities. We secure the grassroots. If you want those votes, I can make sure your platform reaches the people who actually decide elections.”

“I need people who understand the ground game,” Xavier replied, playing his part flawlessly. “I’d like to offer you a part-time campaign internship at Stone & Associates. We’ll give you a desk right in our strategic planning bullpen so we can coordinate the grassroots rollout.”

“I would be honored, Mr. Stone,” Vivian said, extending a hand to seal the deal.

Zoe watched them shake hands. The trap is set, she thought, a cold shiver of anticipation running down her spine.

Three Days Later — The Bullpen

The newly reopened offices of Stone & Associates were quiet, but the underlying tension was thick enough to cut with a knife.

Zoe sat in her new director of operations suite, looking at a dual-monitor setup. On her left screen was a live video feed from a pinhole camera disguised as a smoke detector in the strategic server bullpen. Vivian Fan sat at her new desk, diligently sorting through voter registration spreadsheets.

Xavier had deliberately placed her desk in a quiet, recessed alcove right next to the firm’s localized server cabinet and the campaign archives. To Vivian, it felt like the ultimate inner sanctum—a jackpot position that allowed her unrestricted, late-night access to the firm’s digital infrastructure. To Zoe and Jo, it was a digital fishbowl.

“She’s behaving like a professional,” Jo said, leaning over Zoe’s shoulder. Jo tapped her tablet, showing a real-time log of Vivian’s keystrokes. “No sloppy downloads. She hasn’t touched our server files yet. She knows we’d log an unauthorized USB port breach. She’s waiting for a secure window.”

“Xavier is taking her to lunch at one o’clock,” Zoe said, her eyes fixed on the clock on her screen. “Is the cafe ready?”

“My team rigged the cafe’s public router yesterday,” Jo said with a grim, tight smile. “We set up a rogue access point that mimics the cafe’s secure Wi-Fi. The second she connects, we capture her data packets. But we still need her device-level passcode, otherwise her local files remain fully encrypted.”

On the monitor, Zoe watched Xavier walk into the bullpen, leaning lightly on his cane. He tapped on Vivian’s desk, offering a warm, collegial smile.

“Time for lunch, Vivian. Let’s head down to the bistro on the corner. My treat.”

The Bistro — 1:15 PM

The bistro was bustling with the midday Silicon Valley rush. Rain began to patter softly against the high glass windows, casting a familiar, moody gray light over the mahogany tables.

Zoe sat parked in an unmarked SUV half a block away, headphones pressed to her ears, listening to the live audio transmission. Beside her, Jo was monitoring the rogue Wi-Fi signal on her laptop.

“I really appreciate this, Mr. Stone,” Vivian’s voice came through the feed, accompanied by the background clatter of porcelain plates and silverware.

“Call me Xavier, please,” his voice replied. He set his phone on the table. “Damn. My battery is completely dead. Vivian, do you mind looking up the contact number for Councilman Harris on your phone? I need to confirm our afternoon meeting, but my contacts list is locked behind a dead screen.”

“Of course,” Vivian said.

In the SUV, Jo’s screen suddenly flashed with a prompt: Target Device Active. Connection Established.

“She’s unlocking it,” Jo whispered tensely. “Watch her fingers, Xavier…”

Through the audio, Xavier spoke casually, his voice perfectly level. “Let me write that number down. It’s… seven, three, nine, one… correct?”

Zoe’s heart hammered. Xavier wasn’t reading a phone number. He was verbally reciting her five-digit screen passcode under the guise of repeating the councilman’s office extension.

“Got it,” Jo muttered, rapidly typing 7-3-9-1 into the decryption bypass override on her terminal.

On the audio feed, Xavier’s hand moved. A split-second later, there was the sudden, sharp clink of a water glass tipping over.

“Oh, my god, I am so sorry!” Xavier apologized quickly, the sound of rustling napkins filling the microphone. “The ice caught the edge of my sleeve. Let me get the waiter—your sleeve is soaked.”

“It’s fine, really!” Vivian laughed, though her voice carried a trace of annoyance. “I’ll just run to the restroom and dry it off. I’ll be right back.”

“Take your time,” Xavier said reassuringly.

The second the audio registered Vivian’s retreating footsteps, Jo’s fingers flew across her keyboard. “Device unlocked. Establishing physical cloning link.”

Through a hidden camera pin on Xavier’s lapel, Zoe watched his hands move with blinding, mechanical speed. He grabbed Vivian’s unattended iPhone, plugged a tiny, high-tensile USB-C hardware cloner into the port, and entered the passcode.

On Jo’s monitor, a progress bar erupted: [||||||||||||||||||||] 100% Cloned.

“Forty-five seconds on the dot,” Jo breathed, her face illuminated by the green glow of the transfer confirmation. “He’s disconnecting the drive. Phone is back on the table.”

Zoe let out a long, shuddering breath, her forehead leaning against the cool glass of the SUV window. We have her phone.

The Reveal — Stone & Associates, 4:00 PM

The mahogany desk in Zoe’s office was covered in glowing terminals as Xavier stood behind her, his coat dripping wet from the afternoon rain, his face tight with anticipation. Jo Mercer plugged the cloned drive into a secure, air-gapped forensic workstation.

Zoe’s fingers, wrapped in thin, healing bandages from her harbor capture, hovered over the keyboard. She activated her forensic auditing software, sifting through the cloned file system with the clinical precision of her accounting background.

“Look at this,” Zoe whispered, her eyes reflecting the columns of data scrolling down her screen. “These are local ‘target packages’. She’s been tracking the personal credit card debts of the chief of staff for the Oakland zoning board. She has folders on three different city planners’ extramarital affairs.”

“The national security bureau uses these local folders to build leverage over bright political stars early in their careers,” Xavier said quietly, his voice hard. “They play a decade-long game. But Vivian isn’t storing these files locally. Where is she sending them?”

Suddenly, a red alert banner flashed across Jo’s monitoring terminal.

“We have a live breach,” Jo announced, her voice rising in pitch. “Vivian’s bullpen desktop just connected to a virtual private network. She’s logging into an encrypted cloud portal.”

“She’s uploading Xavier’s target package,” Zoe realized, her fingers flying to route Jo’s spyware exploit directly into Vivian’s active browser session. “The physical keylogger we installed yesterday is capturing her keystrokes in real-time. Jo, get the decryption keys!”

The screen flashed as a string of complex, alphanumeric ciphers were captured by the backdoor exploit. On Zoe’s main monitor, Vivian’s browser window was mirrored in high resolution.

The encrypted cloud portal opened.

But it wasn’t a standard consulate server. The hosting IP address bypassed the San Francisco chancellery entirely, routing through a private Swiss bank server bank owned directly by Victor Stark’s offshore shell companies.

Zoe scrolled down, her breath catching in her throat, the color draining from her face as the master database unlocked.

“Xavier…” Zoe gasped, her hands shaking so violently she had to pull them away from the keyboard. “This… this is insane. Look at these names.”

On the screen, organized in pristine, clinical digital folders, was a horrifying global repository of kompromat and illicit influence peddling. Zoe scrolled through index after index featuring the private files of:

  • Tech Billionaires & Founders: Meticulously tracked private flight coordinates, offshore fund wire receipts, and hidden camera footage.
  • Former Presidents & Prime Ministers: Hand-signed correspondence, private calendars detailing unmonitored visits to private islands, and unverified, devastating dossiers of extramarital encounters.
  • U.S. Municipal Mayors & Local Officials: Meticulous lists of campaign bundling, illegal foreign national donations, and hotel registration files from regional conferences.
  • Royal Families & Global Elites: Intimate, deeply compromising email correspondences and hidden photographs that could bring down entire royal lines of succession.

“It’s a honeypot,” Xavier whispered, his knuckles turning white as he gripped the handle of his cane, his eyes reflecting the glow of the massive, corrupt network on the screen. “Victor Stark was never just a financier. He was the domestic architect of a global blackmail apparatus. Vivian Fan wasn’t just building local campaign portfolios—she was feeding the raw intelligence that keeps Stark’s empire of kompromat alive.”

Zoe looked from the glowing screen to Xavier’s haunted face. The shadow in the Bay wasn’t a localized political dispute. They had just unlocked the keys to a vast, international shadow state that held the world’s most powerful leaders by their throats.

“Wendy didn’t just stumble onto a campus spy ring,” Zoe said, her voice dropping into a terrified, resolute whisper. “She found the master key to Stark’s entire network. And they killed her for it.”

She looked at Xavier, her jaw set, her eyes burning with an absolute, uncompromising resolve.

“We are going to burn his entire empire to the ground.”
