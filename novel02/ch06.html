Scene 6: The Choice at the Precipice

Setting: Jo Mercer’s dark office in West Oakland. The speakerphone has just clicked dead. Zoe sits frozen in the heavy plastic zip-ties, her heart hammering against her ribs as she tries to reconcile the cold, unfeeling boss she thought she knew with the desperate protector who just promised to walk into a death trap for her.

MATCH CUT TO:

Ten Minutes Earlier — Xavier Stone’s Office

The rain was a sheets-of-lead deluge clawing at the panoramic windows of Stone & Associates. Inside the darkened drafting studio, the only light came from the glowing screen of Xavier’s laptop, casting a harsh, pale blue light across his face.

On the screen, a digital folder lay open. It was a duplicate of the encrypted file Jo Mercer had just routed to him: audio recordings of the Aegis Exterior Maintenance crew talking about framing Yian, alongside a low-resolution drone video showing silhouetted figures breaching Wendy’s sixth-floor window.

Xavier’s desk phone suddenly vibrated, its harsh ring shattering the silence. He snatched the receiver before the first ring could end.

"Mercer. Report," he said, his voice tight.

What came through the speaker wasn't Jo’s usual confident, sarcastic drawl. It was a ragged, blood-choked gasp, followed by the gravelly voice of the harbor gang’s leader.

“Stone. We have Mercer. We have the girl Zoe. Bring the master files to her office in ten minutes, or we open her carotid artery right here. Just like we did to her brother's little reporter friend.”

Xavier’s chest seized, a cold, violent wave of adrenaline flooding his veins. He looked at the face-down photograph of his dead younger brother.

"I'm coming," Xavier said, his voice dropping into a flat, deadly whisper. "Don't touch her. I have the files. I'm coming."

He slammed the phone down, grabbed his keys, and snatched a black encrypted flash drive from his computer port.

"Xavier, stop!"

Lesley Croft stepped out from the shadows of the doorway, her face pale, her knuckles white as she clutched the office doorframe. She had heard the speakerphone. She had heard the gravelly voice threatening Zoe's life.

"What are you doing?" Lesley cried, her voice rising in panic. "You can't go down there alone! They are killers, Xavier. They killed Wendy! Call the police. Call 911! Let a SWAT team handle this!"

Xavier didn't stop. He threw on his dark cashmere overcoat, his movements mechanical, cold, and rapid. "I can't call the police, Lesley."

"Why?" Lesley screamed, stepping in front of the door to block his exit, tears of frustration streaming down her face. "Why do you always have to play the martyr? This is what the police are for! They have hostage negotiators, they have tactical units—"

"The police are the ones who put Yian in a cell within six hours of the murder without looking at a single alternative suspect!" Xavier barked, his icy composure finally cracking as he grabbed Lesley by the shoulders, his voice echoing off the concrete walls. "You think this is a normal robbery, Lesley? Wendy was murdered because she discovered Vivian Fan's network—the student APAPA leader who was building 'target packages' for a foreign civilian intelligence agency. She was a textbook access agent."

He released his grip, his breathing ragged as he stared down at the floor.

"The gang on Wendy’s roof—they aren't street thugs. They are the physical cleanup crew for a corrupt transpacific political apparatus that goes all the way up to the local chancellery. Douglas already warned me about the sudden 'Attorney's Eyes Only' restriction on the precinct’s CCTV logs. It’s a systemic lockdown. In the real world of intelligence, when a local attorney is told a target 'belongs to intelligence' or is 'above their pay grade,' they back off and let the state bury the bodies. If I call the Oakland PD dispatch, there is a very high probability the responding officers report to the exact same handlers pulling Vivian's strings."

Lesley shook her head, terrified by the scale of the shadow he was describing. "Even if... even if the local cops are dirty, Xavier, what about federal agents? Call the FBI!"

"The gang gave me ten minutes, Lesley!" Xavier’s voice dropped back into a quiet, trembling register. "Ten minutes. If a single police siren, a single blue light, or a single unmarked federal car taps the brakes on that block, those men won't negotiate. They will slice Zoe's throat, use their tactical climbing harnesses to slide down the rear elevator shafts, and disappear into the shipping yards before the first cruiser doors can open. I cannot risk her life on a tactical response time."

Lesley stared at him, her voice dropping into a desperate whisper. "But they'll kill you, too. You're walking into a slaughterhouse, Xavier."

Xavier slowly reached down to his desk. He picked up the silver-plated photograph of his younger brother, turning it over to look at his gentle, smiling eyes one last time.

"Five years ago," Xavier whispered, his voice thick with a lifetime of unshed grief, "when my brother begged me from behind that prison glass to get him out, I told him to trust the system. I told him to let the lawyers negotiate, let the investigators do their jobs, and let the process work. I occupied myself with my new company, thinking the professionals would handle it."

A single, bitter tear rolled down Xavier’s cheek, catching the blue light of the laptop.

"I brought a birthday cake to a county morgue, Lesley," he said, his knuckles turning white as he set the frame back down. "I trusted the boundaries of the law, and it killed my brother. I swore to God I would never let a loved one’s life depend on a system's timeline again. I’d rather die in that harbor than stand on a police line waiting for a negotiator to fail."

He stepped past her, pushing open the heavy glass doors of the office, stepping out into the cold, driving rain, leaving Lesley Croft alone in the dark office, clutching the ledger of a bankrupt firm.

FADE BACK TO:

Jo Mercer’s Office — The Present

The heavy footsteps of Xavier Stone echoed up the wooden stairs of the locksmith shop.

Zoe sat in the dark, the zip-ties cutting into her wrists, her eyes fixed on the shattered doorway. The shadows of the gang members shifted as the floorboards creaked.

Xavier Stone stepped through the threshold, wet, exhausted, holding the silver flash drive tightly in his hand. He didn't look at the knives or the guns pointed at his chest. His eyes locked onto Zoe’s tear-stained face, and for the first time since she had met him, his eyes were filled with an absolute, heartbreaking warmth.

"I'm here, Zoe," he said quietly. "You're going to be okay."
