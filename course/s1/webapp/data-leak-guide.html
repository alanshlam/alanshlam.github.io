Data Leak Response

Guidelines for Individuals & SMEs (Updated Dec 2025)

For Individuals: Protect Your Identity

If you lost your phone, laptop, or suspect hacking, follow these steps immediately (first 24 hours).

1
Detection & Analysis

Spot signs: Slow performance, ransom notes, weird bank transactions.
Tool: Check logs, run ESET Online Scanner.

2
Containment

Isolate device: Disconnect Wi-Fi/LAN. Do not power off (forensics).
Change all passwords immediately using a clean device.

3
Protection

Freeze credit cards. Notify banks. If HKID is lost, report to Immigration Dept.
Resource: Use HKPF Scameter+ for fraud checks.

4
Voluntary Reporting

Notify PCPD voluntarily if personal data is at risk (id theft). Report to Police if theft involved.

For SMEs: Business & Client Protection

SMEs (5-50 staff) must act fast to protect client trust and minimize PDPO risks.

Phase 1: Immediate Response (0-24 Hrs)

  • 🔴 Isolate: Disconnect affected segment from network. Don't reboot servers.
  • 🔍 Assess: Map leaked data (Credit cards? Client HKIDs?). Use logs.
  • 📞 Triage: Call HKCERT Hotline (8105 6060) for technical help.

Phase 2: Notification & Compliance

💡 Notification Strategy:
  • Clients: Notify immediately if risk is high (e.g., credit cards leaked). Be transparent.
  • PCPD: Voluntary but highly recommended "as soon as practicable" (aim for < 5 days).
  • Police: Report to Cyber Security Bureau (CSTCB) if hacking/criminality suspected.

Phase 3: Recovery

Restore from clean Offline Backups (3-2-1 rule). Patch vulnerabilities. Anonymize data per 2025 PCPD Guide.

⚖️ Am I Legally Liable? (Dec 2025 Status)

Understanding the consequences of NOT reporting a data breach in Hong Kong.

!
Reporting to PCPD (Privacy Commissioner)

Status: Voluntary (Not mandatory yet).

Risk: If you don't report and PCPD finds out (e.g., client complaint), you face stricter scrutiny. Breaching DPP4 (Security) can lead to Enforcement Notices. Non-compliance with notices = Fine HK$50k + 2 Years Jail.

!
Reporting to Police (HKPF)

Status: No general obligation for accidental loss.

Risk: Strongly encouraged if criminal (Hacking/Theft). Helps in civil defense. If "Serious Crime" involved, withholding info could theoretically be obstruction (rare for SMEs).

!
Civil Liability

Clients can sue for damages (e.g., fraud loss due to leak) under PDPO Section 66. Prompt notification helps mitigate these claims.
