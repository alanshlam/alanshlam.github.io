A Note Left on the Basalt Desk

To: Elena
From: Ruby

If you are reading this, it means you have finally stepped past the gantry, ignored the port roads, and let the sea fog lead you up the cliff steps. Welcome to your room, Elena.

Sean and I spent weeks arguing over the architecture of this wing. He wanted to reinforce the foundation with enough military-grade stabilization pylons to withstand an orbital strike, but I insisted on letting the raw basalt stone speak for itself. I left this rear wall unpolished so you could feel the real weight of the mountain behind you, dry and warm from the geothermal siphons.

If you look out the panoramic glass toward the bay, you’ll see the tide is currently rolling in. At night, the glass tints automatically to block the glare, and if you leave the screen slid back just an inch, you can hear the rhythmic, deep-blue pulse of the bioluminescent kelp forests in the shallows below. It is a slow, steady tide—a far cry from the violent, artificial flashing of the Capital’s security corridors or the sterile, bleach-scented labs of the CDC.

On the corner of the desk, I have set out a complete set of your favorite biological reference journals, salvaged from the old Althea archives. The desk itself is crafted from old-growth timber, and Sean personally helped me install the passive copper Faraday mesh behind the wood. When you sit here, your scanners and terminals will find only a clean, quiet silence. No networks can reach you here. No handlers can trace your queries. The world cannot demand your mind for its wars anymore.

There is a fresh pot of herbal tea warming on the stove in the central pavilion, and Sean is currently on the lower terrace trying to convince a stubborn birch sapling to grow straight against the coastal wind. Take a long, uninterrupted breath of this sea air, slide your shoes off, and rest.

You spent years running toward the fires the rest of the galaxy ignored. Your fight is done, Elena. Your home is built.

With all my love,

Ruby
