Act VII: The Slag Analysis

Director Vance stood in the absolute dark of the core server vault, the cold beams of three tactical flashlights cutting through a heavy, acrid fog of vaporized plastics. The room was deathly quiet, save for the dripping of industrial coolant fluid hitting the floor plates. It smelled of burned copper, burnt rubber, and iron.

"Where is the forensic sweep team?" Vance’s voice was dangerously flat, his silhouette rigid against the ruins of his multi-million-dollar facility.

"Outside, Director," replied Special Agent Carter, stepping over a puddle of soot-stained water. He pointed his flashlight at the center of the room. "But they aren't going to find anything to swab. There is no biological data left. There is no digital data left. Look at the primary rack."

The central server enclosure—a military-grade, armor-shielded cabinet that had housed the entire PROJECT_NEMESIS database—was unrecognizable. It looked as though a miniature volcano had erupted directly inside the housing. The top-level core switches were gone, replaced by a vertical, smoking cavity that sliced straight down through twenty layers of blade servers.

"This wasn't an explosive device," Carter said, adjusting his respirator mask. "A concussive blast would have tripped our overpressure sensors and triggered the localized Halon gas suppression systems. The firewalls would have isolated the data. This was purely chemical thermal penetration."

"Thermite?" Vance stepped closer, squinting into the glowing, cherry-red ruins of the main storage matrix.

"Industrial-grade magnesium composite," Carter corrected, aiming his light at the ceiling directly above the ruined rack. "An airborne infiltration asset entered through the high-velocity CRAC cooling ducts. It bypassed the entire corridor security grid by crawling through the infrastructure. Once it reached the cold aisle, it positioned itself directly over the main network switches and ignited."

Carter pulled a pair of long titanium forceps from his forensic kit and reached into the molten, cooled slag at the base of the cabinet. With a sharp crunch, he pulled out a small, twisted lump of carbon-fiber skin fused to a heavily scorched, nickel-alloy plate.

"Look at the flow pattern of the iron," Carter explained, tracing the hardened drips with the tip of his forceps. "The liquid metal didn't blast outward; it flowed downward under pure gravity. It cascaded from the top switch through every single solid-state drive in the array, melting the NAND flash storage chips at three thousand degrees. Our own air-conditioning system acted as a forge blower, feeding oxygen straight into the chemical flame. It accelerated the burn. They used our own cooling system to turn the server room into an incinerator."

Vance ran a gloved hand over the charred edge of the cabinet. "And the system logs? The physical hard-wired backup drives?"

"Melted into a single block of silicon and copper slag on the floor," Carter said flatly. "Every transaction ledger, every modified DNA record, every backup tape cartridge in the archive safe—completely wiped. The physical structure of the data arrays has been altered at the atomic level. It’s the neatest digital execution I’ve ever seen."

Vance turned back toward the dark corridor, his jaw clenched, the amber light of his flashlight reflecting off his cold eyes.

"This wasn't a standard corporate extraction," Vance muttered, his voice echoing in the ruined sever room. "A common hacker copies the file and leaves. Whoever did this didn't just want the drive, Carter. They wanted to make sure we couldn't rebuild the template. They knew exactly how our automated firewalls would react, and they built a machine that knew how to commit suicide to preserve their anonymity."

"What are your orders, Director?"

Vance checked his encrypted satellite device. "The international summit begins in six days. The targets are already moving. Clean the wreckage, isolate the delivery logs for every cleanroom fixture replaced this month, and trace that carbon-fiber chassis. I want to know who engineered a ghost that can execute a self-destruct script before my network can even scream."
